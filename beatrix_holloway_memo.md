# LinenLane Q4 2024 Analysis: Summary Memo

# To: Beatrix Holloway, VP Marketing, LinenLane From: Kenya Harvey Date: October 21, 2026

## Executive Summary

Making Variant B the default email is the clearest win: it earned about $2.51 in revenue per email versus $2.33 for Variant A. Damaged products are the biggest source of returns (313 returns, about $31,531 in refunds), and November revenue ran about 73% above October. I recommend fixing the damage problem, planning promotions and stock ahead of November, and switching to Variant B.

## Findings

New customers peak in the holiday season. New customers placed 397 orders in 2024, more than any other segment, and 145 of them came in October through December. That suggests the holiday season is when LinenLane gets the most orders from new customers.
Bedding is the largest category. The top three categories are Bedding ($51,776.37), Kitchen ($29,415.15), and Bath ($23,788.12). Bedding alone brought in 36% of revenue, so revenue depends heavily on Bedding.
November is the strongest month. November revenue ($23,170.90) was about 73% higher than October (13,423.48). November is the month to plan promotions and inventory around.
Damaged products drive the most returns. Damaged products were the top return reason, with 313 returns and about $31,531 in refunds, or roughly $101 per return. Reducing damage would cut refunds more than fixing any other return reason.

## The A/B Test Result

Variant B converted at 2.10% versus 1.85% for Variant A. That's about 25 more purchases for every 10,000 emails. The difference is unlikely to be a coincidence. I recommend making Variant B the default email. Its average purchase was slightly lower, but more purchases more than made up for it, giving about $2.51 in revenue per email versus $2.33. The lift is likely real, though it could be as small as a couple of extra purchases per 10,000 emails, so I'd keep monitoring it.

## Recommendations
Review packaging and shipping for damaged products. Damaged products were the top return reason, with 313 returns and about $31,531 in refunds.
Plan promotions and stock up ahead of November. November revenue was about 73% higher than October, and 145 of New customers' 397 orders came in October through December.
Make Variant B the default email. It earned about $2.51 in revenue per email versus $2.33 for Variant A.

## Data Quality Notes

Before analysis, I audited the data. Return reasons appeared under six spellings (for example "Damaged" and "Damaged.", "Late delivery" and "late delivery"), which I consolidated into three categories: Damaged, Late delivery, and Not as described. This changed which reason ranked first, so the damaged-products finding relies on the cleaned data. Three SKUs in the inventory tab had been converted to dates; I flagged them and left them as is, since SKUs aren't used in any analysis. I checked the date columns and found no inconsistencies.

## Limitations and Next Steps

This analysis covers 2024 sales, returns, customers, and marketing, so it can't show whether November's jump repeats each year or why products are being damaged. The email test was a single campaign, and the lift could be as small as a couple of extra purchases per 10,000 emails. A follow-up would add prior-year sales, shipping and carrier data on damaged orders, and a longer email test to confirm the lift.
